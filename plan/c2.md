# C2: German speech to text on one workstation

Test design, written 2026-10-09 and committed before any C2 run. Shared
rules: `plan/c0-common.md`. A change after the commit goes into a new dated
section at the end.

## Question

How accurately and how fast do open speech recognition models transcribe
read German speech on one RTX 4090, and how much faster than real time?

## Why it matters for a German SME

Meeting minutes, dictation and call notes are the most common first wish
for in-house AI, and audio is exactly the data a company does not want to
send to a US service. BKS-Lab already transcribes its own meetings locally
(whisper.cpp on a worker host); C2 gives that choice a measured basis and
shows a customer what one card can process per day.

## Data

- FLEURS, `google/fleurs`, config `de_de`, split `test`, revision
  `70bb2e84b976b7e960aa89f1c648e09c59f894dd`, licence CC BY 4.0 (card,
  read 2026-10-09). 862 utterances, read Wikipedia sentences (FLORES
  sentences), 16 kHz.
- All 862 are used, no sampling. Reference text: the `transcription` field
  (the dataset's normalised form). Several speakers read the same sentence,
  so the bootstrap resamples sentence ids (`id`), not utterances.
- Audio is not committed. `cases/c2/` holds the utterance ids, durations
  and a SHA-256 per audio file.
- Exposure: FLEURS is public since 2022 and is a standard evaluation set;
  NVIDIA lists FLEURS among its evaluation sets, and the training data of
  the Whisper models is not published. Read Wikipedia sentences are easier
  than meetings; the test measures the floor of the error, not a meeting.

Not used: Common Voice de. Its distribution moved from Hugging Face to the
Mozilla Data Collective with its own access terms (the
`mozilla-foundation/common_voice_17_0` card no longer carries a licence
field, read 2026-10-09), and FLEURS alone is enough for a clean,
repeatable number. A meeting-style set (spontaneous speech, several
speakers) is wanted later; no public German one with a clear licence was
found for this design.

## Method

One utterance per call (batch 1) for latency and real-time factor; then a
second pass with batch 16 for throughput (faster-whisper `BatchedInferencePipeline`,
NeMo `transcribe(batch_size=16)`). Transcripts of the batch-1 pass are
scored.

Fixed text normaliser, applied to reference and hypothesis alike, committed
as `bench/lib/norm_de.py` before the first run: Unicode NFKC, `str.lower()`
(not casefold, so `ß` stays), every character of Unicode category P
replaced by a space, whitespace collapsed. Numbers are not spelled out:
"1990" against "neunzehnhundertneunzig" is an error for every arm alike,
and the share of such cases is reported.

## Metrics

- Primary: corpus WER (jiwer, Apache-2.0) over all 862 utterances.
- Secondary: CER; WER per arm after the normaliser with digits mapped by
  `num2words` (de), as a sensitivity check only.
- Bootstrap unit: sentence id (cluster).
- Machine: real-time factor at batch 1 (processing seconds / audio
  seconds, median and pooled), audio hours per wall hour at batch 16, peak
  VRAM, GPU energy per audio hour.

## Baseline

No trivial baseline exists for WER. The comparison anchor is
`whisper-large-v3`, the model most installs use.

## Sanity gate

Reference: model card of `nvidia/canary-1b-v2` (model-index, read
2026-10-09): FLEURS `de_de` test WER 4.40 (punctuation and capitalisation
not counted). `canary-1b-v2` runs first; our WER must lie within 1.5
points of 4.40 (the normaliser is ours, not NVIDIA's). The parakeet card
gives 5.04 on the same split; that is reported next to our number but does
not gate.

## Expected GPU time

About 2 h (862 utterances, about 2.5 h of audio, six models at batch 1 and
batch 16; NeMo setup excluded).

## 2026-10-09: preparation notes (before any C2 run)

- Data as designed: 862 utterances, 347 sentence ids (the bootstrap
  clusters). The audio is 3.15 h, not about 2.5 h as estimated above
  (`cases/c2/manifest.json`). 18 utterances are longer than 30 s (up to
  47 s).
- NeMo 3.0.0 installs and runs on native Windows (CPU probe of both NVIDIA
  arms passed), so WSL2 is not needed. It lives in its own venv
  (`D:\bench-cap\.venv-nemo`, torch pinned to the same 2.11.0+cu128) so
  that its dependencies cannot move the faster-whisper venv.
- `canary-1b-v2` returns punctuation and case even when asked for
  `pnc="no"`, so the request is not sent; the fixed normaliser removes
  both, which is what the card's 4.40 counts.
- Audio is read with `soundfile` (FLEURS wavs are 16 kHz mono) and passed
  to faster-whisper as arrays: faster-whisper 1.2.1's PyAV path fails with
  the current PyAV release.
- The two primeline checkpoints are converted once with
  `ct2-transformers-converter` (fp16) by `bench/c2/prefetch.py`; versions
  are in `conversion.json` next to each converted model.
- Batch-16 pass for the faster-whisper arms: utterances are batched across
  files (one padded 30 s window each) straight through the CTranslate2
  model, because `BatchedInferencePipeline` batches chunks of one file and
  FLEURS utterances are one chunk each. The 18 longer utterances go
  through the normal long-form path inside the same timed pass. NeMo arms
  use `transcribe(batch_size=16)`. This pass gives throughput only; its
  WER is kept as a check, not scored.
