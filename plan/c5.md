# C5: translation German to English and English to German

Test design, written 2026-10-09 and committed before any C5 run. Shared
rules: `plan/c0-common.md`. A change after the commit goes into a new dated
section at the end.

## Question

How well do local models translate between German and English in both
directions, and does a general local LLM beat a small dedicated
translation model that needs a fraction of the memory?

## Why it matters for a German SME

Contracts, supplier mails, manuals and tenders cross the German-English
line daily. Translation of confidential documents is a typical reason to
keep the model in house, and a dedicated model of a few hundred megabytes
may be enough, which matters for sizing.

## Data

- FLORES+, `openlanguagedata/flores_plus`, split `devtest`, languages
  `deu_Latn` and `eng_Latn`, revision `e707e62e762a`, licence CC BY-SA 4.0
  (card metadata, read 2026-10-09). The dataset is gated (accept on Hugging
  Face); the account and date of acceptance are recorded. 1,012 sentence
  pairs, all used, both directions (2,024 segments).
- Texts are not committed (gated, ShareAlike, and kept out of the open to
  limit contamination); `cases/c5/` holds ids and a SHA-256 per sentence.
- Exposure: FLORES is the standard MT evaluation set since 2021 and may be
  in training data; its gating exists to limit that.

## Metrics

- Primary: chrF++ (sacrebleu, Apache-2.0, signature recorded) per
  direction.
- Secondary: BLEU (sacrebleu default tokeniser `13a`); COMET with
  `Unbabel/wmt22-comet-da` (`2760a223ac95`, Apache-2.0), which is a learned
  metric model, not an LLM judge, run after all arms on the same GPU.
- Bootstrap unit: sentence; paired bootstrap between arms (sacrebleu
  `--paired-bs`, 1,000 resamples, seed 20261009).
- Machine: segments per second at batch 1 and (dedicated MT only) at
  batch 32, peak VRAM, GPU energy per 1,000 segments.

## Baseline

The source sentence copied unchanged as the "translation" (chrF++ of the
copy), which shows how much of the score is shared names and numbers.

## Sanity gate

Reference: OPUS-MT leaderboard
(https://github.com/Helsinki-NLP/OPUS-MT-leaderboard, file
`models/OPUS-MT-models/de-en/opus-2020-02-26.chrf-scores.txt`, read
2026-10-09): `opus-2020-02-26` de to en on `flores101-devtest` chrF 0.6670
(en to de 0.6363). The Hugging Face `opus-mt-de-en` is that release (its
card links `opus-2020-02-26.zip`). `opus-mt` runs first, with plain chrF
(not chrF++) for this check; it must lie within 1.5 chrF points of 66.70
and 63.63. FLORES+ devtest is a corrected successor of FLORES-101 devtest,
which is why the tolerance is not tighter.

## Expected GPU time

About 3 h (2,024 segments x 7 arms plus COMET).

## 2026-10-12: preparation notes (before any C5 run)

Written after the inputs were frozen and before any C5 arm ran. Nothing
above is changed.

- Access: the gate was accepted on Hugging Face by the bench maintainer's
  account; the first successful download was on 2026-10-12. The files are
  `devtest/deu_Latn.jsonl` and `devtest/eng_Latn.jsonl` (JSON lines, not
  parquet) at revision `e707e62e762a`. They stay in `.cache/c5/` on the Mac
  and in `D:\bench-data\c5` on the workstation. `cases/c5/manifest.json`
  (`bench/c5/freeze.py`) holds both file hashes, the 1,012 ids and one
  SHA-256 per sentence and language; every job checks every sentence.
  Translations are derivatives of the ShareAlike, gated text and are not
  committed either; only scores are.
- Dedicated MT arms run in a venv of their own (`bench/c5/prep-pc.bat`):
  torch 2.11.0 with CUDA 12.8, transformers 4.57.6, sentencepiece and
  sacrebleu 2.6.0, pulled in by unbabel-comet 2.2.7 (which pins transformers
  below 5); the same venv runs the gate and COMET. Generation uses each
  model's own `generation_config` (recorded in `meta.json`), at most 512 new
  tokens; MADLAD-400 in float16 with its target tag (`<2en> `, `<2de> `)
  in front of the source, opus-mt in float32. The scored output is the batch
  1 pass; a second pass at batch 32 gives segments per second and the count
  of segments whose output differs from batch 1.
- LLM arms: one user message, the instruction above, a blank line and the
  source sentence; `num_ctx` 2048, `num_predict` 512, `think` off where the
  model has it; the answer is stripped of surrounding whitespace and taken
  as is. EuroLLM-9B-Instruct as `hf.co/bartowski/EuroLLM-9B-Instruct-GGUF:Q8_0`
  (repository revision `55e5590cc457`, Apache-2.0; the digest is recorded per
  run).
- Sanity gate as a job of its own: `C5-opus-mt` translates, `C5-gate`
  (`bench/c5/gate.py`, CPU) computes plain chrF per direction with sacrebleu
  and exits 3 outside 1.5 points; every other arm depends on `C5-gate`.
- COMET (`bench/c5/comet_score.py`, `Unbabel/wmt22-comet-da` at `2760a223ac95`,
  batch 64) runs as the last C5 job on the GPU over all seven arms and the
  copy baseline. Scoring on the Mac: `bench/c5/score_c5.py` (chrF++, BLEU,
  mean COMET, sacrebleu paired bootstrap with `SACREBLEU_SEED=20261009`).
- Dry runs on the workstation (40 sentences opus-mt, 10 MADLAD, 5 EuroLLM,
  COMET on the opus-mt dry output; not scored): all ran. MADLAD-400 3B took
  1.5 s per segment at batch 1, more than the design assumed, so the
  expected GPU time is about 4 h (2,024 segments x 7 arms plus COMET)
  instead of the 3 h above.
