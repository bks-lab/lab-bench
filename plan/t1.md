# T1: automation rate and calibration

Test design, written 2026-10-09 and committed before any T1 number was
computed. Item T1 of `plan/ROADMAP.md`. No new runs: T1 reads the existing
run 1 rows of route1 and pv1. A change to this design after the commit goes
into a new dated section at the end, never into the text above it.

## Question

What share of cases can a tool decide alone at a target accuracy, and how
trustworthy are its probabilities?

A tool that returns a probability with its answer can be used as a filter:
answers above a threshold are taken as they are, the rest go to a person.
The automation rate is the share of cases that pass the filter while the
passed cases stay at or above the target accuracy. The second part asks
whether a stated 0.9 means right nine times out of ten (calibration).

## Data

All arms, run 1, the files `bench/lib/results.mjs` (`runRows`) selects. No
run is repeated, no row is dropped except as stated here.

- route1 (gold labels in `reference/route1/gold.<lang>.jsonl`), the eight
  arms in `results/route1/`. Items are the gold items an arm has a row for,
  as in `bench/score-route.mjs`. MASSIVE intent items with a single option
  (gold scenario with one intent) are left out: every tool gets them right
  by construction and they would inflate the rate. Pooled set per language:
  - de: MASSIVE scenario plus MASSIVE intent with 2+ options
  - en: the same plus fast-decisions, all single-label tasks
  Each part is also reported on its own.
- pv1 line questions (`is_req`, `must`, `axis`, `evidence`, `level`) of the
  `line` requests, all fourteen arms in `results/pv1/`. Reference: German
  rows against `reference/pv1/adjudicated-a.de.jsonl`, English rows against
  `reference/pv1/claude-c.en.jsonl`, as in the toolmap. `is_req` is scored
  on every line, the four others only on lines the reference calls a
  requirement (the rule of `bench/score.mjs` and `bench/toolmap.mjs`).
  Pooled set per language: all five questions; each question also on its own.
  Project and profile questions are not part of T1.
- English-only GLiNER models (GLiNER2.5-Decide, GLiNER2.5-Decide-1B and
  their variants) are reported on German rows as an extra, never as a
  family's best arm, the rule of the toolmap.

## Probabilities used

- Jev: the `probs` it returns, as given.
- Local LLM: the letter probabilities of the next token, already
  renormalised over the option letters in the rows (`bench/run-local.mjs`).
  The mass the letters had before renormalising (`mass`) is reported per
  arm (median and 10th percentile). A low mass means the model wanted to
  write something else; the renormalised value hides that.
- GLiNER: the classifier's softmax over the offered labels, as given.

Confidence of an item = the probability the tool gives its own answer
(`probs` at the position of `choice`). For almost every row this is the top
probability; where a tool's answer is not its most probable option, the
answer is what would be automated, so its probability is the one used.

Caveat, stated in every report: all three are distributions over the
offered options, not calibrated claims that the answer is right. For
GLiNER this is explicit (`bench/run-gliner.py`): a softmax over labels.
The letter probabilities of a local LLM are the same kind of quantity.
Jev's documentation presents its probabilities as judgments; whether they
are calibrated is exactly what the calibration part measures.

A row without an answer (local arm, questions with more than 26 options,
or a missing letter) counts as wrong and is never automated: confidence
minus infinity. It stays in the denominator of the automation rate and is
left out of the calibration measures (their n is reported).

## Metrics, fixed in advance

Target accuracies: 95 % (headline) and 90 %.

1. Automation rate, in-sample. Sort items by confidence. For every distinct
   confidence value t, take the items with confidence >= t (ties are never
   split: a threshold either passes all items of one value or none). The
   automation rate is the largest share of items, over all such t, whose
   accuracy is at or above the target. If no t reaches the target, the
   rate is 0. The threshold is chosen on the very rows it is scored on, so
   this is an optimistic, in-sample number: it says what a perfectly tuned
   threshold would have passed on this data.
2. Automation rate, 2-fold cross-fitted. Split the items into two halves by
   `line` (all questions of one text in the same half), with a fixed
   seeded shuffle (mulberry32, seed 20261009, over the sorted line ids).
   Choose the threshold on half A by rule 1, apply it to half B: coverage
   and accuracy of the passed B items. Then the other way round. Reported:
   the mean coverage of the two halves, and the accuracy of all passed
   items of both halves together. A half where no threshold reaches the
   target passes nothing. The cross-fitted accuracy can fall below the
   target; that is the point of reporting it.
3. Calibration over answered items:
   - ECE: 10 equal-width bins of confidence, [0, 0.1), ..., [0.9, 1.0]
     (1.0 in the last bin). ECE = sum over bins of (n_bin / n) x
     |accuracy_bin - mean confidence_bin|.
   - Brier score, multi-class: mean over items of the sum over offered
     options of (p_option - y_option)^2, y one-hot on the gold answer
     (range 0 to 2, lower is better).
   - Reliability diagram data: per bin n, mean confidence, accuracy.
4. Baseline: the most-frequent-answer rule of the existing reports. route1:
   per option set (MASSIVE scenario; MASSIVE intent per gold scenario;
   fast-decisions per task), the most frequent gold label on the same rows.
   pv1: per question the most frequent reference answer (`evidence`:
   `none`, the one answer shared by all lines). Its confidence on an item is
   the in-sample share of that label in the item's option set, and its
   automation rate is computed by rule 1. It is 0 unless the majority label
   of some option set alone reaches the target on enough items.

## Outputs

- `bench/score-automation.mjs` (reuses `bench/lib/results.mjs` and
  `bench/lib/route-ex.mjs`) writes `results/route1/automation.md`,
  `results/pv1/automation.md` and the machine file
  `results/<pv>/automation.json`.
- `bench/toolmap.mjs` reads the machine files and adds rows of the new kind
  `automation`: `route1-automation95-de`, `route1-automation95-en`,
  `pv1-automation95-de`, `pv1-automation95-en`. Value = in-sample automation
  rate at 95 % (percent) of the family's best arm on the pooled set;
  baseline = the rate of the most-frequent-answer rule at the same target;
  skill = (value - baseline) / (100 - baseline), which equals value / 100
  when the baseline is 0. The cell also carries the 90 % rate, the
  cross-fitted rates, ECE and Brier. The best arm is chosen after the fact,
  like every toolmap cell. All other toolmap rows stay byte-identical.
- `results/automation-findings.md`: short answer, tables per family and
  language, what it means in practice, caveats. Numbers only from the
  script output.

## Known limits

- One run per arm. route1 run 1 had unshuffled options (`shuffled: false`).
- pv1 references are model judgments (see `results/pv1/crosscheck.md`), so
  "accuracy" on pv1 is agreement with one judge or an adjudicated judge.
- pv1 has 275 lines per language over five questions; per question the
  sets are small and a 95 % threshold rests on few items.
- Choosing the best arm per family after the fact flatters families with
  many arms (GLiNER), as everywhere in the toolmap.

## Addition 2026-10-09: curves for the website chart

Added after the T1 numbers were published, for an interactive chart on the
test bench page. It adds an output and changes no number above.

`bench/score-automation.mjs` also writes `results/<pv>/automation-curves.json`,
per language and arm on the pooled set, aggregates only:

- coverage curve: for every grid point 1 %, 2 %, ..., 100 %, the largest
  prefix of the items ranked by confidence (ties never split, rule 1) whose
  share of all items is not above the grid point, with its coverage and
  accuracy. Unanswered items are never in a prefix.
- target curve: for every target accuracy 80 %, 81 %, ..., 99 %, the
  in-sample automation rate of rule 1. At 95 % it equals `auto95` of
  `automation.json`; the script fails if it does not.
- the same two curves for the most-frequent-answer baseline.

Arms without any probability would be skipped with a note; all current arms
carry probabilities. The file is on the path list of `bench/leak-scan.json`
(until 2026-10-10 `bench/export-allow.json`) like `automation.json`.
