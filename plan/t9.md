# T9: robustness to typos and colloquial wording

Test design, written 2026-10-12 and committed before any T9 run. A change
after the commit goes into a new dated section at the end.

## Question

How much accuracy does a classifier lose when German input is written the
way people type and speak: with keyboard slips, without umlauts, with
colloquial contractions and fillers?

## Why it matters

MASSIVE utterances are clean transcripts. Real tickets, chat messages and
speech-to-text output are not. A model that is accurate on clean text but
breaks on a missing umlaut is a risk an accuracy table does not show.

## Data

`cases/t9/requests.jsonl`, frozen by `bench/t9/perturb.py` (rules, seeds
and lists in its docstring, hashes in `cases/t9/manifest.json`): 1,000
MASSIVE de requests of route1 (seeded sample, `default_rng(20261009)`), each
in four variants with the same two questions and gold:

| variant | rule | utterances changed |
|---|---|---|
| `clean` | unchanged | 0 |
| `typo` | one keyboard edit (QWERTZ neighbour, drop, swap, double) in each word of 4+ letters with probability 0.15, at least one per utterance | 997 |
| `translit` | ä, ö, ü, ß written as ae, oe, ue, ss | 404 |
| `colloquial` | fixed spoken contractions (gibt es -> gibts, nicht -> nich, eine -> ne, ...) and one opening filler (äh, also, hey, sag mal, du) | 1,000 |

4,000 requests, 8,000 questions. The perturbations are rule-based, not
model-generated, so the gold cannot drift; their realism is limited to what
the rules express, and the findings say so. MASSIVE is CC BY 4.0
(`cases/route1/README.md`); the perturbed text is a derivative under the
same licence.

## Arms

The four local arms of T2 (`winnow-12b`, `gemma4-12b`, `qwen3.8-27b`,
`gliner2.5-multi-decide`) with exactly the T2 method, plain option order
only, through `bench/t2/run_route.py --requests cases/t9/requests.jsonl`.
Jev: `bench/run-jev.mjs` cannot read another request file; a Jev arm needs
a `--requests` option first and runs from the Mac on Michael Boiman's go
(hosted API). Not part of this preparation.

## Metrics

- Primary, per arm and variant: accuracy drop against `clean` on the same
  utterances (paired), with a 95 % bootstrap interval, unit utterance (all
  four variants together), 1,000 resamples, `default_rng(20261009)`.
- Secondary: accuracy per variant; flip rate against `clean`; the drop on
  the utterances the variant actually changed (for `translit` 404 of 1,000).
- The clean variant is re-run in the same job (same runtime and load), not
  taken from route1.
- Scorer: `bench/t2/score_t2.py t9`.

## Sanity gate

Shared with T2: the T9 jobs depend on the T2 sanity jobs (port agrees with
route1 run 1 on at least 99 % of 500 questions, per family).

## Expected GPU time

About 55 minutes: 8,000 questions per arm at the route1 median latencies
(Winnow-12B 12 min, Gemma 4 12B 11 min, Qwen3.8 27B 29 min, GLiNER 2 min).
